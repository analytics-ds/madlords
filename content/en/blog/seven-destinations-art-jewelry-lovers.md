## Traveling for jewelry and art

Some cities concentrate a rare cultural density: exceptional museums, artisan workshops, antique markets. Here are seven destinations to favor for those who like to combine travel and refinement.

## Paris, Place Vendome

Five high jewelry houses on a single square: Cartier, Van Cleef, Boucheron, Chaumet, Bulgari. Place Vendome remains the world epicenter of jewelry. To complete with a visit to the Galerie d'Apollon at the Louvre.

## Antwerp, diamond capital

80% of the world's rough diamonds transit through Antwerp. The Diamond District concentrates cutters, brokers and jewelers in a few-street neighborhood. The DIVA Museum traces the history of the stone.

## Jaipur, the pink city

Birthplace of precious stone cutting in India, Jaipur houses workshops where the gestures of stone setting have been transmitted for five centuries. Emeralds and rubies are worked there in conditions impossible to reproduce elsewhere.

## Florence, the Ponte Vecchio

The Ponte Vecchio has been lined with goldsmith shops since the 16th century. Beyond, Florence offers Cellini's masterpieces at the Bargello Museum and the Medici jewels at Pitti Palace.

## London, Mayfair and Bond Street

Mayfair concentrates the English houses (Asprey, Garrard, Boodles) and specialized antique dealers. The Victoria & Albert Museum completes the visit with its European silverware gallery.

## Geneva, auction sales

Sotheby's and Christie's organize in Geneva the most important jewelry sales in the world. Attending a sale, even without buying, gives access to pieces otherwise invisible.

## New York, the Upper East Side

Fifth Avenue concentrates Tiffany, Cartier and Harry Winston, but it is in the Upper East Side that the most interesting specialized antique dealers and contemporary jewelers are found.

## Composing your itinerary

Three cities are enough for a two-week journey. The Paris-Antwerp pair remains the classic base, complemented by Florence or Jaipur depending on whether one seeks ancient European art or the discovery of millennial craftsmanship.

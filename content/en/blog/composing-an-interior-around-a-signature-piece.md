## A single piece can change everything

Composing an interior around a signature piece is a balancing exercise. The point is to let this piece tell its story without crushing the rest of the space, but without losing it in the decor either.

## Identifying the central piece

The signature piece can be a designer armchair, a signed lamp, an antique console or a work of art. The criterion is not size but presence: it must be the first thing to catch the eye when entering the room.

## Breathing space

A signature piece needs air around it. Plan at least 60 cm of clearance on the sides, and avoid framing it with other strong elements. Empty space is as important as the piece itself.

## Dedicated lighting

A directional floor lamp, an integrated ceiling spot, a wall sconce: the signature piece deserves its own light. It changes the perception of the material, reveals the details, and signals to the eye that this element matters.

### Three types of lighting to favor

- **Direct light**: to emphasize shapes (sculpture, graphic furniture).
- **Accent lighting**: to reveal a material (precious wood, stone).
- **Indirect light**: to create an atmosphere around the piece without distorting it.

## Dialogue with the rest of the space

A signature piece does not necessarily marry well with other signature pieces. On the contrary, it often gains by being surrounded by sober elements that highlight it. A designer armchair often finds its place in a clean living room rather than in an accumulation of signed furniture.

## Letting the piece live over time

A signature piece patinas, evolves, catches the light differently with the seasons. Looking at it as a living presence rather than a frozen object helps to build an interior that does not go out of fashion, because it is composed around something durable.
